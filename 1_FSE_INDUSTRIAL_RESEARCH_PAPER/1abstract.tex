\begin{abstract}
Machine learning models for system diagnostics, anomaly detection, performance prediction, and root cause analysis, require large volumes of labeled execution traces for training.
However, collecting production kernel traces is expensive: it imposes runtime overhead, raises privacy concerns, and captures only the narrow slice of behaviors present during collection windows.
This data scarcity bottleneck limits the adoption of ML-based observability tools in production environments.
We present \textsc{SynthTrace}, a diffusion-based framework for generating synthetic kernel execution traces that preserve the statistical and structural properties of real system behavior.
\textsc{SynthTrace} models traces as multi-channel sequences (event types, timestamps, CPU affinity, thread identifiers, and process metadata) and learns to generate coherent sequences through a Transformer-based denoising diffusion process.
To ensure generated traces respect system invariants, we introduce a constraint learning and post-hoc repair mechanism that enforces valid event transitions, temporal bounds, and attribute consistency.
We evaluate \textsc{SynthTrace} on six diverse benchmark workloads spanning CPU-intensive, memory-intensive, and I/O-heavy applications.
In downstream next-event prediction tasks, models trained on repaired synthetic traces achieve 91\% of the F1-score obtained with real data, and mixing synthetic with real traces improves baseline performance by up to 3\%.
Constraint-guided repair consistently improves raw synthetic data quality by 13--18\% across context lengths.
\textsc{SynthTrace} enables practitioners to augment limited real traces with high-fidelity synthetic data, reducing the cost barrier for deploying ML-based system diagnostics.
